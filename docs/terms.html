5. Safety — please read this one

TrailCam is a recording tool. It is not a navigation system and it is not a safety device. It records where you have been. It does not give turn-by-turn directions, does not know whether a path is legal, passable, open, safe or suitable for you, does not detect that you have fallen, and cannot call for help.

Do not operate your phone while moving on difficult ground, or while driving or cycling. Start the recording before you set off and stop it after you arrive; the app is built to record a trip without you touching the screen.

You are responsible for your own route, your own equipment and your own decisions, and for obeying the traffic laws, access rules and land-use restrictions where you are. In remote terrain, carry a map, a compass, a means of calling for help, and enough judgement not to need any of them.

A phone can run out of battery, run out of storage, lose signal, get wet, or be shut down by iOS. Do not put yourself somewhere where the app working is the difference between safe and not.

6. Accuracy — what the numbers are worth

Satellite positioning is an estimate, and every figure TrailCam shows is derived from it. Buildings, tree cover, canyons, tunnels, cliffs and weather all degrade it, sometimes badly. Distance, elevation, climb, pace and the shape of the drawn line are therefore approximations. The place name and weather shown on a trip card come from third-party services looked up once at the trip's starting point, and can be wrong or stale.

Do not use TrailCam's output where an accurate measurement matters: surveying, insurance, legal boundaries, competition results, navigation in terrain you do not know, or anything else where a wrong number has consequences.
