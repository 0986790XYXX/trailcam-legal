5. Safety — please read this one

Roam is not a navigation system and must not be relied on for safety. It records where you have been. It does not give turn-by-turn directions, does not know whether a path is legal, passable, open, safe or suitable for you, and does not call for help.

You are responsible for your own route, your own equipment and your own decisions, and for obeying the traffic laws, access rules and land-use restrictions where you are. In remote terrain, carry a map, a means of communication and enough judgement not to need either.

6. Accuracy — what the numbers are worth

Satellite positioning is an estimate, and every figure Roam shows is derived from it. Buildings, tree cover, tunnels, cliffs and weather conditions all degrade it. Distance, speed, elevation, climb and the shape of the drawn line are therefore approximations, sometimes poor ones. Where the app knows a measurement is unreliable it says so rather than smoothing it away — a gap in the signal is shown as a gap, not bridged with a straight line — but no amount of honesty makes a GPS fix exact. Do not use Roam's output where an accurate measurement matters: surveying, insurance, legal boundaries, competition results, or anything where a wrong number has consequences.
